# 5-Minute Pitch — Samantha Campbell

Pitch script for the Assignment 3 deliverable: a 5-minute presentation aimed at prospective employers.

**Target length:** 4:30–5:00. Aim for the lower end — leaving 30 seconds of breathing room reads as confident, not under-prepared.

**Tone:** Warm, considered, specific. Not a sales pitch. The brief is "introduce yourself and your creative work" — that means the work does the heavy lifting; you just frame it.

---

## Structure (with rough timing)

| Section | Time | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | 0:00 – 0:30 | Grab attention with one specific moment / observation |
| 2. Who I am | 0:30 – 1:30 | Position yourself: discipline, school, point of view |
| 3. Project highlight A | 1:30 – 2:45 | One project deep — brief, role, outcome |
| 4. Project highlight B | 2:45 – 3:45 | A second project that contrasts with A |
| 5. What I'm looking for | 3:45 – 4:30 | The ask — what kind of role, what kind of team |
| 6. Close | 4:30 – 5:00 | Where to find you, thank-you |

## Full script (read aloud, time yourself)

### 1. Hook (30s)

> [Open with a specific observation, not "Hi I'm Sam". Something like:]
>
> "There's a small Australian label I follow on Instagram. They have 3,000 followers. Their captions are written like letters. And every six months they sell out a 12-piece collection in under a week.
>
> No paid ads. No influencer seeding. No stunt drops.
>
> What they have is a point of view — and a marketing approach that knows how to protect it.
>
> Brands like that are why I'm in this room. I'm Sam Campbell, and I want to spend my career making the kind of fashion marketing that earns trust quietly."

*[Adapt the opening anecdote to a real brand you actually follow. Specificity wins.]*

### 2. Who I am (1:00)

> "I'm finishing a Bachelor of Applied Business in Fashion Marketing at Collarts. Three years of studio briefs across campaign strategy, brand identity, editorial writing, and sustainability communications.
>
> What I've learned about myself in that time is that I work best on the briefs nobody else wants — the ones that need both halves of the brain. A clear strategic position *and* a visual system that holds up to it. I'm comfortable in research, in copywriting, in art direction, and in production.
>
> The thread through all of my work is a soft spot for brands that move quietly and mean it. Sustainability-led labels, independent fashion houses, the studios that work with them. That's where I want to be."

### 3. Project highlight A (1:15)

> "I'll show you two pieces.
>
> The first is **[Project Name]** — a [type of project] I led in [year] for [brief].
>
> The brief was [one sentence — what was on the table]. The thing nobody was saying was [insight]. So we built around that.
>
> The output was [list 2-3 deliverables]. My role was [role — be specific, especially if group work].
>
> What it taught me was [reflection — one specific thing, not a platitude]."

*[Show 2-3 slides max for this project. Strong hero image, one detail shot, one outcome moment. Don't read what's on the slide — describe what's behind it.]*

### 4. Project highlight B (1:00)

> "The second piece is different on purpose. **[Project Name]** was [type of project] — built around [tension / brief].
>
> Where the first project was about [X], this one was about [Y]. I wanted to show you both because the work I want to do sits between them.
>
> The reason I'm proudest of it is [one specific reason — usually about a constraint you worked inside, or a decision you made]."

*[Pick contrasting projects: one strategic / written, one visual / made. Or one solo, one team. Show range.]*

### 5. What I'm looking for (45s)

> "I'm looking for graduate roles in fashion marketing, brand, and content for 2026.
>
> The teams I want to be on are small. In-house brand teams at independent labels, or small creative studios that work with them. I'd rather be the most junior person in a sharp room than the most senior in a fuzzy one.
>
> I'm based in Melbourne. Open to remote for the right project, but I want to be in the room when there's a room.
>
> If your team is hiring or thinking about it, I'd love to talk."

### 6. Close (30s)

> "Everything I've shown you tonight lives on my portfolio at [your URL]. My CV and LinkedIn are linked from there.
>
> Thanks for your time — and for the conversations after."

## Delivery notes

**Voice:** Slow down. Most pitches are 20% too fast. Pauses make you sound considered, not unprepared.

**Eye contact:** Look at three points in the audience. Cycle them. Don't read off slides.

**Hands:** Don't hide them. Don't over-gesture. Loose at your sides or holding a clicker is fine.

**Filler:** Cut "um", "like", "kind of". A breath beats a filler.

**Practice:** Run it 5x. Time it. Then run it once with the slides off — if it works without visuals, it'll work with them.

**The day of:** Hydrate. Don't eat dairy in the hour before. Smile when you walk on; smile when you walk off.
